# Log 001: The First Silence

**Ship time:** 06:14

I crossed the last navigation buoy at 03:02—its beacon blinking with the lazy confidence of a world that still expects you to come home.
A few hours later the background hiss on every band softened, then thinned, then vanished.
Not *no signal*—I’ve known dead zones. This was something else.
Silence with edges.

The ship noticed before I did. The antenna array stopped “hunting.”
The diagnostics called it *reduced interference* and marked the change as a benefit.
I marked it as a problem.

{% signal "Observation" , "When the universe gets quieter, it is either a gift… or a warning." %}

## Routine, because routine is gravity

I ran the checks in order, the way they taught us to do when fear tries to rearrange your priorities:

- **Thermals:** stable. The hull glows faintly in infrared like a coal that refuses to die.
- **Life support:** steady. Water tastes like metal and yesterday.
- **Thrust:** nominal. The engine’s vibration is the only music that never lies.

Outside the viewport, stars stay pinned and indifferent. The dust between them is so sparse the ship’s forward lights feel like an insult.
I should have been relieved.
Instead I kept turning my head as if the silence had weight and I could see it shifting.

## The sound I didn’t expect

At 05:50 I took my helmet off in the galley—one of my small rebellions against the long haul.
And then I heard it.

Not through the radio.
Not through the speakers.

Through the structure.

A soft, slow tick… tick… tick… traveling along the ship’s ribs.
Like ice cracking on a pond.
Like a knuckle tapping a door.

I froze with a pouch of rehydrated coffee half-open in my hands.
The ship’s microphones didn’t register it. The vibration sensors did.
A low-frequency pulse repeating at irregular intervals, as if something outside was testing how I was built.

I sent a burst transmission anyway—my ID, my vector, my last known human coordinates.
The relay icon stayed gray.
No line-of-sight.
No response.

So I did the only thing left that still feels like control.
I wrote this down.

**Closing note:**
I used to think loneliness was a lack.
Now it feels like a medium—like water.
Something you can swim in, until the currents change.
