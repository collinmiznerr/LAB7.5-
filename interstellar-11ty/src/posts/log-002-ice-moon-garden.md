# Log 002: The Garden Under Ice

**Ship time:** 19:03

The silence followed me into the KX-19 system like a shadow that learned my stride.
I picked the third moon because it looked dead: bright ice, no atmosphere worth naming, and fractures that suggested a tired interior.
Dead things are predictable.

I was wrong.

## Descent

On approach, the surface reflected my running lights back at me in clean, cold sheets.
Cracks webbed the south pole like someone had dropped a glass sphere and watched it split.
I set down near the widest fault line and listened to the hull cool.

The moment I opened the airlock, my suit’s audio filtered in the familiar faint roar of nothing.
A vacuum has a personality if you spend enough days talking to it.

I anchored a line, then lowered myself into the fracture.
The ice walls were blue-black and layered like old bruises.
My headlamp found trapped bubbles—ancient breaths that never got to be lungs.

## The seam

At seventeen meters down the temperature rose by a few degrees.
At twenty-one, my visor fogged.
At twenty-eight, I smelled it through the filters: minerals, damp stone, and something faintly sweet.

The fracture opened into a chamber.
Not carved, not blasted—*grown*, the way coral grows a city out of patience.

Bioluminescent strands hung from the ceiling like roots searching for a planet.
They pulsed slowly, in the same irregular rhythm as the ticking I felt in the ship.
Tick… tick… tick…

In the center of the chamber sat an arrangement of stones.
Not random.
A spiral.
Each piece placed with an attention that made my throat tighten.

I didn’t touch it.
I circled it like it might wake up.

## The message without words

My suit’s sensors recorded heat signatures in the strands—gentle and stable.
Not animal.
Not machine.
Something between, or something that makes that distinction look childish.

When my headlamp swept across the spiral, the strands brightened.
They didn’t move toward me.
They didn’t recoil.
They simply… acknowledged.

A thought arrived in my mind with the softness of a memory:

> **You are loud.**

Not in sound.
In presence.
In insistence.

I stood very still, because I suddenly understood that my heartbeat was a broadcast.

I backed away.
Not out of fear—out of respect.
And because I wasn’t sure if the silence I’d been tracking was protecting me…

…or protecting *this place* from me.

**Closing note:**
I left a single object at the fracture mouth: a small pine patch from training.
A symbol of “something that grows.”

If anyone else comes here, I want them to know:
someone found beauty under the ice,
and decided it was worth leaving untouched.
